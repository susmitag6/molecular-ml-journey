# Medicinal Chemistry Learning Journal — Lesson 23
**Date:** 29 October 2026  
**Topic:** Solvation, Desolvation, and the Role of Water in Drug Binding

### Summary

Today I learned that **water plays an active role in protein–ligand binding** and significantly influences binding affinity.

- **Solvation:** Ligands and proteins interact with surrounding water molecules, forming hydration shells.
- **Desolvation:** During binding, some ligand–water and protein–water interactions are disrupted or reorganized.
- **Hydrogen bonding:** Forming a new protein–ligand hydrogen bond does not necessarily improve affinity because favorable interactions with water may be lost.
- **Binding thermodynamics:** Binding depends on the overall free-energy balance, including solvent reorganization and changes in molecular entropy.
- **Statistical physics connection:** Water molecules fluctuate among many configurations, so a single static protein–ligand structure cannot capture their full thermodynamic contribution.
- **Molecular dynamics connection:** MD simulations can help investigate hydration patterns, water residence, and protein–ligand interactions over time.

### Key Takeaway

**Strong protein–ligand interactions alone do not guarantee strong binding affinity. Solvation, desolvation, water reorganization, and entropy must also be considered.**
