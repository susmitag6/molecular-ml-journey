**Lesson 22: Noncovalent Interactions in Drug Binding**

## 1. Main Concept

Protein–ligand binding is governed by the balance of favorable and unfavorable interactions between the ligand, protein, and surrounding solvent.

The five key contributions discussed were:

1. **Hydrogen bonding:** Directional interactions that can contribute to binding specificity.
2. **Hydrophobic interactions:** Burial of nonpolar surfaces, often accompanied by water reorganization.
3. **Electrostatic interactions:** Attractions and repulsions between charged or polar groups, influenced by solvent and local environment.
4. **van der Waals interactions:** Short-range dispersion and steric effects that contribute to molecular recognition and shape complementarity.
5. **Solvation and entropy:** Changes in hydration, molecular flexibility, and configurational freedom during binding.

## 2. Thermodynamic Foundation

Protein–ligand binding follows:

$$
P + L \rightleftharpoons PL
$$

The standard binding free energy is related to the dissociation constant:

$$
\Delta G^\circ_{\mathrm{bind}} = RT\ln(K_d/C^\circ)
$$

where \(C^\circ\) is the standard concentration.

The free-energy balance is:

$$
\Delta G = \Delta H - T\Delta S
$$

Binding affinity therefore depends on both enthalpic and entropic effects, not simply the number of favorable molecular contacts.

## 3. Important Scientific Insight

**More hydrogen bonds do not necessarily mean stronger binding.**

A new hydrogen bond with the protein may require breaking favorable ligand–water interactions or restricting ligand conformations.

Consequently, a ligand forming fewer direct protein contacts can sometimes bind more strongly if its overall free-energy balance is more favorable.

## 4. Connection to Molecular Dynamics

Molecular dynamics simulations can help investigate:

- Hydrogen-bond occupancy and persistence.
- Protein–ligand contact distances and probabilities.
- Water-mediated interactions.
- Ligand conformational flexibility.
- Protein binding-pocket rearrangements.

These measurements provide mechanistic insight, but they are not equivalent to direct calculations of binding free energy.

## 6. Key Takeaway

**Binding affinity reflects the free-energy difference between bound and unbound states, including solvent and entropy—not merely the strength or number of direct protein–ligand interactions.**
