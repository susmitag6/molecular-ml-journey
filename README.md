# Molecular ML Journey: From Molecular Simulation to Data-Driven Molecular Modeling

This repository documents my week-by-week journey into molecular machine learning and data-driven molecular modeling.

With a background in physics and extensive experience in biomolecular simulations and Soft Matter, I am particularly interested in the intersection of physics, computation, and data. 
My goal is to build on my experience in molecular dynamics, statistical mechanics, and kinetic modeling while developing practical skills in cheminformatics, molecular machine learning, and ML-guided molecular simulation.

This repository will document what I learn, what I build, the questions I encounter, and how I connect new machine-learning concepts with my existing understanding of molecular systems.

This is an evolving journey toward applying physics-based and data-driven approaches to complex molecular problems.

# My Goals and Research Direction

## My Overall Direction

My goal is to develop expertise in **AI-driven molecular and atomistic modeling**, building on my background in statistical physics, molecular dynamics (MD), Markov State Models (MSMs), and complex dynamical systems.

I want to combine physics-based simulation with machine learning to understand molecular structure, dynamics, free-energy landscapes, and material or biological properties.

My research interests span both **biomolecular science and materials science**. Rather than choosing one field prematurely, I will focus on transferable computational methods that are useful in both.

## Short-Term Goal — My Current Four-Month Plan

My immediate goal is to build a strong foundation in molecular machine learning and integrate it with my existing simulation expertise.

**Month 1 — Molecular Machine Learning**

Learn cheminformatics, molecular representations, QSAR, model validation, PyTorch, and graph neural networks. Develop a reproducible AChE bioactivity prediction project.

**Month 2 — Machine Learning for Molecular Dynamics**

Explore trajectory representations, slow collective variables, VAMP/VAMPnets, and ML-guided adaptive sampling. Connect these methods to my existing experience with MD, MSMs, and complex dynamics.

**Month 3 — Protein–Ligand Modeling and Free Energies**

Study protein–ligand interactions, binding thermodynamics, relative binding free-energy calculations, and uncertainty. Develop an understanding of how machine learning and physics-based calculations complement each other.

**Month 4 — Integration and Scientific Portfolio**

Integrate molecular ML, simulation, uncertainty, and active learning into reproducible research projects. Consolidate my results into a scientific portfolio.

I will follow this plan without introducing major new subjects that distract from completing it.

## Keeping Both Research Directions Open

Although my current learning projects focus primarily on biomolecular applications, the underlying methods are transferable.

**Biomolecular research:** Protein conformational dynamics, molecular recognition, binding free energies, enhanced sampling, and ML-guided molecular design.

**Materials research:** Soft matter, polymers, glassy dynamics, atomistic simulation, phase behavior, transport, and machine-learned interatomic potentials.

My existing experience connects to both areas. I will use the four-month plan to strengthen my shared methodological foundation before deciding whether to specialize further.

## Long-Term Goal

My long-term goal is to work toward **generative AI and AI-driven multiscale modeling for molecular and materials science**.

I want to develop expertise in geometric and equivariant deep learning, generative models, machine-learned interatomic potentials, electronic-structure methods, and AI-guided simulation.

I am particularly interested in connecting microscopic interactions to molecular dynamics, free-energy landscapes, collective behavior, and experimentally observable properties.

## My Guiding Principles

- Build on my existing expertise rather than restart in an unrelated field.
- Learn the physics behind ML methods, not just how to use software packages.
- Develop methods that can transfer between biomolecular and materials systems.
- Use ML to complement, accelerate, and guide physics-based simulation.
- Prioritize scientific understanding, careful validation, uncertainty, and reproducibility.
- Complete my current four-month plan before pursuing advanced generative AI or DFT in depth.

**My direction in one sentence:**

From statistical physics and molecular simulation toward molecular machine learning, AI-guided atomistic simulation, and eventually generative AI and multiscale modeling—with both biomolecular and materials research remaining open.
